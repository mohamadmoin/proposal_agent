# Systematic review / meta-analysis protocol (PRISMA-P)

When to read: after choosing a systematic review design.
How to use: there is no participant sample size; the methods describe the review process. Register in PROSPERO.

Essentials of the protocol
- Question in PICO/PECO form; eligibility criteria (designs, populations, outcomes, languages, dates).
- Information sources (PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, and for Iran SID, Magiran, IranDoc) and a full
  search strategy for at least one database.
- Selection by two independent reviewers, disagreement resolution, PRISMA flow diagram.
- Data extraction form, piloted, two reviewers; risk-of-bias tool matched to the designs (RoB 2, ROBINS-I, NOS,
  JBI for prevalence).
- Synthesis: effect measure, random-effects model, heterogeneity (I², tau²), subgroup and sensitivity analyses,
  publication bias (funnel, Egger) when ≥ 10 studies; certainty of evidence (GRADE).
- Target population: define the primary pooled estimate for one population (e.g. the general population); pool
  clinical or symptomatic samples (dyspepsia, endoscopy patients) separately or as a subgroup, never into the main
  national estimate.
- Search date: the search runs up to the protocol's search date (this year), stated as a planned date. Do not
  promise an expected number of eligible studies unless a pilot search gives it; prior reviews' counts are a
  reasonable guide when cited.
- Gap: when the rationale is that earlier reviews are outdated, name the most recent one found (find_studies) and
  what it lacks.
- Handling of studies with several diagnostic tests (choose one per study by a stated hierarchy), and the regions or
  provinces in each subgroup.
- PROSPERO registration before screening, stated the same way in the methods and the ethics.
- Publication-bias tests for single-arm proportions are of limited validity: use them cautiously (e.g. Doi plot or
  funnel with Egger, interpreted with care).
