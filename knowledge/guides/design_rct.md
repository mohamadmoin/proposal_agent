# Randomised controlled trial (SPIRIT for the protocol, CONSORT for reporting)

When to read: after choosing a randomised trial, before the sample size and the methods.
How to use: every item below must be explicit in the protocol; record_decision for the intervention, comparator,
primary outcome and its time point.

Essentials of the protocol
- Trial design (parallel, crossover, cluster), allocation ratio, superiority or non-inferiority.
- Participants: eligibility with thresholds (age, disease definition, lab ranges, e.g. HbA1c 7–10%, 25(OH)D < 20 ng/mL
  when the effect is expected only in deficient people), setting and centres.
- Intervention and comparator in full: agent, dose, route, frequency, duration, who delivers it, adherence monitoring;
  placebo identical in appearance when blinded. "Placebo or usual care" is not a comparator.
- Primary outcome with its time point (e.g. HbA1c change at week 12) and secondary outcomes; adverse events.
- Randomisation: sequence generation (software, block size, stratification factors), allocation concealment (central
  or sealed opaque envelopes), who enrols and who assigns. Describe it once and consistently.
- Blinding: who is blinded and how.
- Sample size: two means or two proportions for the primary outcome; the difference is the minimal clinically
  important difference or a conservative pooled estimate (meta-analysis), not the largest effect of one small trial;
  SD from a comparable population; attrition.
- Analysis: intention-to-treat as primary (all randomised, analysed as allocated), per-protocol as sensitivity;
  ANCOVA adjusted for baseline; handling of missing data; interim analyses if any.
- Registration (IRCT for Iran) and the ethics code before enrolment; data monitoring and stopping rules for risky
  interventions.

Pitfalls seen in reviews
- Dose or duration not fixed.
- Exclusions defined by post-randomisation events, or adherence thresholds that contradict ITT.
- Effect size borrowed from one small optimistic trial; underpowered trial.
