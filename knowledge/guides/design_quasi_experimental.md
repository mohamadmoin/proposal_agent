# Quasi-experimental (non-randomised) intervention study (TREND)

When to read: after choosing a non-randomised intervention design (pre-post, controlled before-after, interrupted
time series), before the sample size and the methods.
How to use: decide the comparison strategy first (record_decision), then write the methods around it.

Essentials of the protocol
- Comparison: a concurrent control group or an interrupted time series is much stronger than a single-group pre-post;
  if single-group, state the threats (secular trends, regression to the mean, Hawthorne effect) and how they are
  reduced (several baseline points, blinded observers, same season).
- Unit of delivery and unit of analysis (ward, nurse, observation opportunity). When observations are clustered within
  people or wards, use a design effect in the sample size and cluster-aware analysis (GEE, mixed models).
- Intervention described fully (content, sessions, duration, who delivers, fidelity checks).
- Outcome measurement: instrument, observers, inter-rater reliability (kappa on a meaningful sample), timing.
- Ethics of observation: overt observation with consent is the default; covert observation needs explicit ethical
  justification and committee approval, and cannot coexist with individual written consent obtained beforehand.
- Sample size: two proportions or means (pre vs post), parameters from a comparable setting, DEFF for clustering;
  attrition only applies to people, not to observation opportunities.
- Analysis: pre-post comparison with the right test for paired or clustered data; segmented regression for ITS;
  adjusted models.

Pitfalls seen in reviews
- Parameters from a different population (e.g. neonatal ICU for an adult ICU study).
- Individual-level objectives that the chosen anonymous measurement cannot answer.
- Clustering ignored.
