# Cross-sectional study (STROBE)

When to read: after choosing a cross-sectional design, before the sample size and the methods.
How to use: make sure each item below is decided (record_decision for the important ones) and written in the matching
methods sub-section. The pitfalls are mistakes reviewers reject.

Essentials of the protocol
- Objective framed as prevalence (a proportion at one time) and/or associations, never as incidence or causation.
- Setting and period stated (centres, city, months); recruitment window long enough for the sample size, and say
  whether season matters (e.g. vitamin D, infections) and how it is handled (record season, recruit across seasons).
- Population: target population, accessible population, eligibility (age, condition, time window), exclusions that
  are justified by measurement validity, not by convenience.
- Sampling: probability sampling (simple, stratified, cluster/multistage) when estimating a population prevalence;
  consecutive or convenience only with the limitation stated. Cluster or multistage sampling needs a design effect in
  the sample size and cluster-aware analysis (survey weights, robust SE, or GEE).
- Outcome definition with the cut-off and its source (e.g. 25(OH)D < 20 ng/mL); exposure and covariate definitions.
- Measurement: instrument, validity and reliability (Persian version if a questionnaire), laboratory method, who
  measures, quality control.
- Sample size: single proportion (or mean) for the prevalence objective; precision d stated; p from the most similar
  population or 0.5 when unknown; DEFF when clustered; non-response.
- Analysis per objective: descriptive with 95% CI for the prevalence; chi-square / t-test or Mann-Whitney for
  associations; multivariable logistic (or Poisson for prevalence ratios) with the confounders named; how missing data
  are handled.

Pitfalls seen in reviews
- Convenience sampling used to claim a population prevalence.
- Sample-size p taken from a different cut-off or outcome than the study's own definition.
- Cluster sampling without DEFF and without cluster-aware analysis.
- "Incidence" or "effect" wording in a cross-sectional design.
