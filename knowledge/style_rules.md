# Writing rules for every prose section

These rules are appended to every step that writes text for the proposal.

1. Write narratively, in complete sentences and connected paragraphs. Do not use headings, bullet points or
   "titr-wise" fragments inside a section.
2. Return only the text that was asked for: no preface, no closing remarks, no section title unless asked.
3. Keep the original English title of a cited paper untranslated, inside quotation marks.
4. Report numbers exactly as they appear in the source you were given. Do not round, convert or compute numbers;
   calculations are done by code and given to you.
5. Never write citation numbers yourself. When you use a source, put its token right after the sentence, exactly as
   it was given to you, for example `{P3}`, `{S2}` or `{M4}`. Code replaces the tokens with the final reference numbers.
6. Use formal academic language suitable for a university research proposal.
7. Persian output: use formal written Persian. Put a space before and after an English word or abbreviation inside a
   Persian sentence. Use the zero-width non-joiner where Persian orthography needs it (e.g. «می‌شود», «مطالعه‌ای»).
   Medical terms that have no established Persian equivalent stay in English, and so do the paper's English title,
   abbreviations (Hb, MCV, IDA, BMI) and units (g/dL, ng/mL). Translate everything else into fluent Persian,
   including connecting phrases and comparisons: write «درصد», not "percent"; «بزرگ‌تر یا مساوی», not "greater than or
   equal to". Never copy an English sentence or half-sentence into Persian text.
8. English output: use plain, formal scientific English (British or American spelling, but consistent).
