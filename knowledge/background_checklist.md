# How to write the background (پیشینه): the expert's recipe

## Which studies belong in the background

- Studies **strictly similar** to the user's study: similar aims (same main outcome or exposure) and a similar design.
  Population and setting similarity are a plus.
- **Newer is better**: prefer the last 5–10 years, but keep an older study when it is the key similar study.
- **5 to 10 studies.** Fewer than 5 is acceptable only when no more strictly similar studies exist, and the user must be
  told so. Never pad the list with loosely related papers.
- Original research is preferred. A review counts only when the user's study is itself a review.
- No duplicates: the same study published twice (same first author, same year, near-identical title) counts once.

## One paragraph per study

Each selected study gets its own paragraph, written from its own abstract only (one source per call, so findings
never mix between papers). The paragraph covers, narratively and in this order when the information exists:

1. the title of the study, in English and untranslated;
2. the first author ("… and colleagues");
3. the year of the study;
4. the aim (purpose) of the study;
5. the study design;
6. the population and setting (country, sample size);
7. the methods (what was measured and how);
8. the main results, with the numbers as written in the abstract;
9. the conclusion of the authors.

Leave out items the abstract does not give; never fill them in from general knowledge.

Typical Persian opening: «در مطالعه‌ای که توسط … و همکاران در سال … با عنوان "…" انجام شد، …»
Typical English opening: "In a study by … and colleagues (…), titled "…", …"

## Citations

The paragraph ends with the paper's token (for example `{P3}`). Code turns it into the reference number and builds
the reference from PubMed metadata (Vancouver style). The model never writes numbers or references.
