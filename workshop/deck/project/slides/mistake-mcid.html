Lab notebook · mistake 3 · sample size

A correct calculation asked a student for 10,386 women

We assumed

The difference a previous study observed is the difference to detect. Cohort title: postpartum depression after caesarean vs vaginal birth.

The run showed

p₁ = 16.7%, p₂ = 14.7% from an Iranian study → 5,193 per group, 10,386 in total. Arithmetic: correct. As a thesis: impossible.

Why it was wrong

A study should be powered for the smallest clinically important difference — a clinical decision — not for whatever one study happened to see. n grows with 1/Δ²: a 2-point gap needs thousands.

The fix

The number stays as computed, but above 2,000 the calculation asks the researcher to check the smallest important difference. It is an amber step: you decide, not the model.
